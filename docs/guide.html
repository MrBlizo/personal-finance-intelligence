Personal Finance user guide

Everything Personal Finance does, on the PC and on the phone. Each section is short: read the one you need. In the Windows app, press Ctrl+K and type a few letters to jump to any page.

First steps

The first time you open Personal Finance, a short assistant asks for your language, your main currency, your accounts and whether you want a starter budget. You can change every answer later in Settings.

  1. Add your accounts: checking, savings, cash, cards, loans and investments, each with its own currency.
  2. Bring in your history: import a statement from your bank, or move over from YNAB, Cashew or Mint.
  3. Open Month by month to see where the money went.

Month by month

Pick a month on the strip at the top. Personal Finance shows what you earned, spent, paid (bills and card or loan payments) and saved (transfers into savings and investment accounts), where the money went compared with the month before, your spending pace through the month, and the biggest expenses. On the phone, tap Income or Expenses on Home, or the Month by month card.

Adding transactions

Press Ctrl+N anywhere on the PC, or the big + on the phone. The amount field accepts sums such as 12.50+3, the payee remembers its last category, and the last account is preselected. On Android you can also long-press the app icon or add the Personal Finance widget to the home screen.

Scanning receipts

On the PC: open Quick add and drop a photo of the receipt on the dashed box (or click it). Windows' own text recognition reads the total, the date and the shop; nothing leaves your PC. The photo is attached to the transaction. If Windows says no recognition language is installed, add one in Settings › Time & language › Language & region.

On the phone: in the transaction editor, tap the scan icon at the top, then take a photo or choose one. The phone reads it offline and fills in the form.

Always check the amount before saving. Personal Finance looks for the line that says Total, Net à payer, Summe or المجموع; when there is none it takes the largest amount and tells you so.

Importing bank statements

Personal Finance reads CSV, OFX/QFX, QIF and CAMT.053 files. Many US, Swiss, European and Moroccan bank layouts are recognised automatically; for others you choose the columns once and Personal Finance remembers them. Rows that were imported before are skipped, and possible duplicates are shown side by side so you can decide.

On the phone: Accounts › import icon, choose the CSV file, check the columns Personal Finance guessed and the preview, then import. New rows wait in the review list.

See also: How to import a CAMT.053 statement.

Moving from another app

  • YNAB: in YNAB choose Export Plan and upload the zip in Import › Move from YNAB. Accounts, categories, transactions and monthly assignments come over.
  • Cashew: in Cashew open Settings › Export data › Export CSV, then use Import › Move from Cashew or Mint.
  • Mint: use the transactions.csv you exported from Mint or Credit Karma, in the same place.

Personal Finance shows what will be created before anything is saved, and importing the same file twice adds nothing.

Budgets

Choose between simple monthly limits per category and envelope budgeting (give every dollar a job) in Settings. With envelopes you assign money to categories each month, move money when one is overspent and set targets that Personal Finance can fund in one click. On the phone, tap a category to assign, move or fund it.

What if and financial independence

On the Forecast page, What if…? lets you try a purchase, a raise or a price change (for example rent +10%) and shows your balance with and without it, with a plain verdict: affordable, tight or not yet. Financial independence estimates when your investments could pay for your spending, from your last 12 months. Both are projections from your own history, not promises or advice.

Year report and taxes

Reports › Year in review shows the year's income, spending, savings rate, months, categories and top payees, and downloads a PDF. Under For your tax return, mark the categories that matter where you live (donations, medical, childcare…). Tax export (CSV) then lists every matching transaction for your accountant.

Phone and sync

  1. On the PC: Settings › Sync › choose your OneDrive, Dropbox or Google Drive folder and a passphrase.
  2. On the phone: Settings › Set up sync › the same service and account.
  3. Enter the same passphrase. Everything is encrypted before it leaves each device; there is no Personal Finance server.

If both devices changed the same item before they synced, Personal Finance keeps the newest change and lists the other one in Settings › Changed on two devices, where you can switch with one click.

Automatic phone updates with Obtainium

The Android app is not on Google Play. To get updates automatically, install Obtainium, tap Add app and paste https://github.com/MrBlizo/personal-finance-intelligence. Obtainium then installs each new APK from the GitHub releases. Without it, Personal Finance shows a card on Home when a newer version is out.

Reminders and updates

The phone reminds you the day before a bill is due and tells you when a category you budgeted for is overspent. Turn either off in Settings › Notifications. The PC checks once a day whether a newer version is out (the Microsoft Store version updates itself); only the version number is fetched.

Keeping your data safe

  • A backup is made every week, and before every restore. Choose a OneDrive folder or a USB drive in Settings › Backup folder and protect backups with a password.
  • Settings › Check my data looks for transfers with a missing side, splits that don't add up and other problems, and links you straight to the transactions concerned.
  • Updates never touch your data: every version since 0.4 is upgraded automatically, and this is tested on real databases from each release.
